# Scheduling & Work-Queue Optimization for AI Agent Fleets: State-of-the-Art Survey

*Date: 2026-07-13 | Method: WebSearch + WebFetch (no exa/firecrawl MCP configured), ~30 queries, ~15 primary sources deep-read | Scope: 2024-07 through 2026-07*

Context for this survey: Gas City + Beads runs pull-claim per-pool queues (atomic CAS). An 11-week trace replay showed priority-band + FCFS-within-band captures nearly all ordering gain (+2% flow time overall, +10% in contended pools); a 4-feature weighted index added +0.1% and was rejected. The queued v2 design (bead dr-fol): hard constraints for feasibility, lexicographic objective (deadlines > value+unlock > makespan > risk > cost > age), full-dependency-graph awareness, rolling horizon with event re-solve, OR-Tools CP-SAT core. Prior-art steals already identified: K8s filter/score, HTCondor ClassAds, Airflow `weight_rule=downstream`, Slurm backfill/fair-share.

The survey ruthlessly distinguishes "has a task queue" from "does scheduling optimization" (an objective + a policy that reorders/allocates against it). Most of the market has the former and markets it as the latter.

---

## Executive Summary

1. **NEW-SINCE-SURVEY — Temporal shipped real queue scheduling.** Task Queue **Priority** (integer 1-5, higher first) and **Fairness** (fairness keys + weighted round-robin across keys) went Public Preview in 2025 and **GA on 2026-05-05** ([changelog](https://temporal.io/changelog/priority-fairness-generally-available), [docs](https://docs.temporal.io/develop/task-queue-priority-fairness)). This is the only major production orchestrator that moved beyond FIFO-with-concurrency-limits, and its design (few static bands + fairness across keys, no scoring function) independently corroborates our replay finding that priority-band + FCFS captures nearly all the gain.

2. **GAP-CONFIRMED — no agent-fleet product does scheduling optimization.** Devin (MultiDevin / Devin Desktop, 2026-06), Cursor background agents, Factory.ai droids/Missions, OpenAI Codex cloud (3-5 queued parallel tasks), Claude Code dynamic workflows (16 concurrent / 1,000 subagents per run, 2026-05), GitHub Agent HQ mission control (2025-10), OpenHands Cloud, Blitzy ("thousands of agents in parallel"): all are parallel dispatch + kanban/cron + concurrency caps. None publish an objective function, a solver, preemption, or even priority queues at the fleet level. The "coordinator decides who does what" is an LLM planner, not a scheduler.

3. **KNOWN (with new details worth stealing) — K8s batch scheduling matured but stayed job-shaped.** Kueue (fair sharing via cohorts, priority preemption, topology-aware scheduling, MultiKueue; 2026 roadmap adds cooperative preemption for checkpointable workloads), Volcano v1.11-v1.13 (2025: network-topology-aware gang scheduling, queue DRF), YuniKorn (hierarchical queues, quota preemption ≥1.8), Armada (multi-cluster DRF). The genuinely new agent-specific K8s work is **sandbox pooling** (kubernetes-sigs/agent-sandbox CRD, Volcano AgentCube warm pools, 2026), i.e. capacity supply, not dispatch optimization. Two steals identified beyond our list (§Design Changes).

4. **NEW-SINCE-SURVEY — a real 2025-26 academic literature on agent-aware scheduling now exists, but it lives almost entirely at the GPU/serving layer.** Autellix (PLAS/ATLAS program-level attained-service, 4-15x throughput, arXiv 2502.13965), LLMSched (uncertainty-aware DAG scheduling of compound LLM apps, 14-79% JCT reduction, arXiv 2504.03444), SAGA (workflow-atomic GPU scheduling, 1.64x TCT, arXiv 2605.00528), Justitia (virtual-time fair queuing for task-parallel agents, arXiv 2510.17015), Dyserve (per-workflow ILP over heterogeneous backends, arXiv 2607.02942). "Treat the agent program, not the request, as the schedulable unit" is now a validated research direction — one layer below ours.

5. **NEW-SINCE-SURVEY — routing research is crossing into queue scheduling.** CQB-MNL contextual **queueing** bandits jointly learn model routing AND queue prioritization from implicit feedback (arXiv 2602.02061, Feb 2026); SmartLLMs Scheduler puts **predicted per-query success rate** into a cost-performance scheduling objective (arXiv 2508.03258). RouteLLM/Martian/Not-Diamond remain strictly per-request model choice. None of these touch dependency-graph work queues, but the routing+queueing union means our model-tier routing and stochastic-success terms have nearby academic anchors now.

6. **GAP-CONFIRMED (all four claimed net-new territories), each with a nearest neighbor to steal from:** (a) dependency-graph-aware dispatch of agent *work items* — nearest: LLMSched DAGs and Airflow downstream-weighting, nothing at the issue/bead level; (b) human-review-bandwidth as a scheduled resource — the bottleneck is now loudly named industry-wide, and MSR'26 "Early-Stage Prediction of Review Effort in AI-Generated Pull Requests" (AUC 0.96; 69% of high-effort PRs caught at a 20% review budget) provides the predictor, but nobody schedules dispatch against reviewer capacity; (c) context-quality-aware dispatch — only serving-level cache-locality analogs (Preble, Mooncake, SAGA KV-reuse); (d) stochastic agent-success-probability in the objective — SmartLLMs/router literature does it per-request; no fleet work-queue does.

7. **GAP-CONFIRMED — no published scheduling-policy numbers on real agent-fleet work-item traces.** Published numbers are serving-level (Autellix 4-15x, LLMSched 14-79% JCT, SAGA 1.64x) or HPC-job-level (digital-twin replay studies), measured where queueing delay dominates. Nothing comparable to an 11-week coding-agent fleet replay measuring flow-time deltas of ordering policies exists in public. Our +2%/+10% result has no external benchmark to compare against and would itself be a first-of-kind publication.

8. **Design verdict: nothing shipped supersedes dr-fol; three findings should adjust it** (details §Design Changes): steal Trigger.dev's priority-as-time-offset for starvation-free aging, elevate Temporal-style fairness keys for contended pools (where our replay found the 10% gain), and adopt Dyserve's pre-solve/off-critical-path pattern for the CP-SAT loop. The small measured headroom (+2%) plus the industry's uniform convergence on bands+fairness argues for keeping the v2 solver's *ordering* ambitions modest and spending its complexity budget on the feasibility layer and the resource models (review bandwidth, exclusive resources) instead.

## Q1. Agent-fleet orchestrators: who ships real scheduling?

Classification key: **[OPT]** = scheduling optimization (objective + reordering/allocation policy). **[QUEUE]** = task queue with concurrency/cron/limits only. **[PLANNER]** = LLM decides assignment (no algorithmic scheduler).

- **Agent-specific K8s (new, 2026)**: **kubernetes-sigs/agent-sandbox** CRD for isolated stateful singleton agent runtimes, pre-warmed pools, sub-second start on GKE/gVisor ([k8s blog 2026-03-20](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2026/03/20/running-agents-on-kubernetes-with-agent-sandbox/), [roadmap](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/agent-sandbox/blob/main/roadmap.md)). **Q2 answer:** nothing here does agent-work-item dispatch, but two mechanisms map cleanly onto our design beyond the K8s filter/score steal we already listed: (1) Kueue's **two-phase admission** (reserve quota under hard constraints, then run pluggable admission checks) is structurally identical to our "hard constraints determine feasibility" layer and gives a battle-tested decomposition; (2) **cohort borrowing with fair-share reclaim** is a proven model for letting idle pool capacity flow to hot pools while remaining reclaimable, which our per-pool CAS claiming has no answer for today. Gang scheduling is largely irrelevant at bead granularity (our work items are single-worker), but becomes relevant if v2 ever schedules multi-agent molecules (formula steps) as atomic units; Volcano/Kueue show the all-or-nothing admission pattern for that.

## Q3. Academic 2024-2026

### Serving-layer agent-aware scheduling (the dominant thread)

- **Autellix** (arXiv [2502.13965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13965), 2025-02): treats agentic *programs* as first-class schedulable entities; PLAS (single-thread) / ATLAS (multi-thread DAG) non-clairvoyant policies prioritize LLM calls by the program's cumulative attained service; 4-15x throughput vs vLLM at equal latency. The canonical "program-level scheduling" paper.
- **LLMSched** (arXiv [2504.03444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03444), 2025-04): uncertainty-aware scheduling of compound LLM applications; DAG model with stage-level duration uncertainty, Bayesian-network profiling, entropy-based identification of uncertainty-reducing stages; 14-79% mean JCT reduction vs SOTA in sim + testbed.
- **SAGA** (arXiv [2605.00528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00528), 2026-05): workflow-atomic GPU scheduling; Agent Execution Graphs predicting KV-cache reuse, session-affinity batching with work stealing, "Agent Fair Share" metric; 1.64x geo-mean task-completion-time vs vLLM 0.15.1 on 64 GPUs, at ~30% lower peak throughput.
- **Justitia** (arXiv [2510.17015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17015), 2025-10, rev 2026-03): virtual-time fair queuing for task-parallel agents on shared GPU servers; memory-centric cost model; "selective pampering" = prioritize agents that would finish first under idealized fair sharing.
- **Dyserve** (arXiv [2607.02942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02942), 2026-07): per-workflow **ILP** choosing per-node model + verifier over a heterogeneous backend pool; pre-solves at multiple pressure levels at admission so the solver stays off the critical path; residual re-solve on tool failure preserving completed work.
- Adjacent serving work: **Parrot** (OSDI'24, semantic variables/DAG-aware), **Throughput-Optimal Scheduling for LLM Inference and AI Agents** (arXiv [2504.07347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07347), queueing-theoretic stability analysis), **Semantic Scheduling** (arXiv [2506.12204](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12204v1)), **Agent.xpu** (arXiv [2506.24045](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.24045v1), heterogeneous SoC), **AugServe** (arXiv 2512.04013, expected-cost two-stage scheduling), **AgentServeSim** (arXiv [2606.09613](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09613), hardware-aware multi-turn agent serving simulator with tool-schedule replay), **AIOS** (arXiv [2403.16971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16971), COLM 2025: "agent OS" with a pluggable FIFO/RR scheduler — architecture, not optimization).
- Position/theory: **"From Agent Loops to Structured Graphs: A Scheduler-Theoretic Framework for LLM Agent Execution"** (arXiv [2604.11378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11378), 2026-04): analyzes agent loops as degenerate "single ready unit" schedulers, argues for explicit static DAGs + immutable plans + layered recovery across 70 surveyed systems. Position paper, no system. Independently lands on our graph-aware thesis.

### Model routing (and its crossover into queue scheduling)

- **RouteLLM** (arXiv [2406.18665](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.18665)), **Martian**, **Not-Diamond**, **Unify**: per-request model choice by predicted quality/cost. No queue awareness. Ecosystem catalog: [Not-Diamond/awesome-ai-model-routing](https://github.com/Not-Diamond/awesome-ai-model-routing). 2025-26 evolution: bandit/online routers (BaRP, PILOT, Router-R1, MasRouter for multi-agent routing).
- **CQB-MNL / ACQB** (arXiv [2602.02061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02061), 2026-02): **the first paper found that does routing AND queue scheduling jointly** — contextual queueing bandits with MNL feedback from user retrials; Thompson sampling + decaying forced exploration; Õ(√t) routing regret with queue-stability guarantees.
- **SmartLLMs Scheduler** (arXiv [2508.03258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03258), 2025-08): schedules queries across LLMs to maximize average job success rate per cost, using per-query+per-model ML predictors with dynamic feedback; evaluated on log parsing + code generation.
- **Token-budget line**: TALE (arXiv [2412.18547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18547), reasoning budgets), **Token-Budget-Aware Pool Routing** (arXiv [2604.09613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09613), 2026-04: estimates each request's token budget and routes to short-pool vs long-pool vLLM fleets; 17-39% fewer GPU instances on Azure/LMSYS traces). Budget-aware *placement*, not work-queue ordering.

**Q3 answer:** the field converged on our layer boundary from below. Everything schedules LLM calls/steps onto GPUs or model pools; nothing schedules *work items* (issues/beads) onto *agent workers*. The techniques (attained-service priority, DAG uncertainty profiling, admission-time pre-solving, queueing bandits) transfer; the problem instance does not exist in the literature.

## Q4. Claimed net-new territory: verify or refute

**(a) Dependency-graph-aware dispatch for agent work items — GAP CONFIRMED.**
Nearest neighbors, none at the work-item level: LLMSched/Dyserve/Parrot DAGs (LLM-call granularity); Airflow `weight_rule=downstream` (static DAG weight, already on our steal list); OpenHands Large Codebase SDK "orchestrates changes in the correct order" (topological safety, no optimization, marketing-documented only, [openhands.dev](https://www.openhands.dev/)); Blitzy's dependency-aware knowledge graph (feeds decomposition, undocumented scheduling). No published system reads a full work-item dependency graph and optimizes dispatch order/unlock value across an agent fleet.

**(b) Human-review-bandwidth as a scheduled resource — GAP CONFIRMED, with a strong adjacent result to steal.**
The bottleneck itself is now the loudest theme in the space: 85% of surveyed engineers say review is the new bottleneck; PR review time +91% while AI-assisted devs merge 98% more PRs ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/merge-gate-coding-agents/), [dev.to review-bottleneck](https://dev.to/code-board/the-review-bottleneck-why-more-ai-code-means-slower-teams-in-2026-1e5n), and a 2x-mandate longitudinal study, arXiv [2607.01904](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01904)). Responses published so far are triage lanes and risk tiers, not capacity scheduling. The key adjacent result: **"Early-Stage Prediction of Review Effort in AI-Generated Pull Requests"** (MSR'26, arXiv [2601.00753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00753)): creation-time prediction of high-maintenance PRs (AUC 0.96), gated triage capturing 69% of high-effort PRs at a 20% review budget, plus the "ghosting/attention tax" characterization. That is a *predictor and a gate*, not a scheduler: nobody models reviewer capacity as a consumable resource in a dispatch objective. Older crowdsourcing/task-routing literature (pre-2024) modeled human worker capacity but not for agent-generated code review.

**(c) Context-quality-aware dispatch — GAP CONFIRMED.**
The only "context-aware" scheduling in the literature is cache-locality at the serving layer: Preble (ICLR'25, prompt-prefix-aware scheduling switching between prefix-hit and load-aware routing), Mooncake (FAST'25, KV-cache-centric disaggregation), SAGA's KV-reuse prediction, locality-aware fair scheduling (2025-01), TokenLake (arXiv [2508.17219](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17219v1)). These optimize *reuse of identical context bytes*, not *quality/fitness of context for a task*. Dyserve's "skill-conditioned profiles" (per-node model/verifier choice conditioned on skill type) is the closest conceptual cousin to dispatch-on-fitness. Nothing dispatches work items based on which worker/session holds the best context for them.

**(d) Stochastic agent-success-probability in the objective — GAP CONFIRMED at the queue level; occupied at the per-request level.**
Per-request: the entire router literature (RouteLLM et al.) plus SmartLLMs (explicit predicted-success-rate objective) plus Early-Stage Review Effort (success/effort prediction for gating). Uncertainty in *scheduling*: LLMSched (stage-duration uncertainty, entropy-driven), AugServe (expected-cost estimates). Nobody puts P(agent completes bead successfully) into a work-queue objective with retry/rework consequences. Caveat for the dr-fol design doc: the per-request occupancy means "stochastic success in dispatch" cannot be claimed as wholly novel — the novel part is specifically its use in fleet work-queue ordering with rework-cost propagation.

## Q5. Published numbers on scheduling-policy gains for agent fleets

**GAP CONFIRMED: no published work measures scheduling-policy deltas on real agent-fleet work-item traces.** What exists, and why it doesn't compare:

| Result | Layer | Number | Why not comparable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Autellix (2502.13965) | GPU serving | 4-15x throughput at equal latency | Measures HoL-blocking removal in LLM call queues under saturation; queueing delay dominates service time |
| LLMSched (2504.03444) | Compound-app scheduling | 14-79% mean JCT reduction | Sim + testbed on synthetic compound apps, not production traces |
| SAGA (2605.00528) | GPU cluster | 1.64x TCT geo-mean, 99.2% SLO | Workflow-atomicity + cache reuse gains, 30% peak-throughput cost |
| Token-budget pool routing (2604.09613) | Pool placement | 17-39% fewer GPU instances | Capacity sizing on Azure/LMSYS request traces |
| Early-stage review effort (2601.00753, MSR'26) | Review triage | AUC 0.96; 69% of high-effort PRs at 20% budget | Prediction quality, not flow-time from a policy |
| HPC digital twins (2508.20016), TARE (2607.04935) | HPC job replay | Policy comparisons (FCFS/backfill/priority) on real logs | Right methodology, wrong domain (HPC jobs) |

The serving papers report multiplicative wins because they operate where queueing delay is most of latency. Our +2% overall / +10% contended-pool flow-time deltas measure ordering gains where *service time dominates and pools are mostly uncontended* — structurally a different regime, and consistent: our contended pools show 5x the gain of the fleet average, and a fully saturated GPU queue shows 4-15x. The 11-week replay is, as far as this survey could find, the first published-or-publishable measurement of ordering-policy value at the work-item level of an agent fleet. Per house policy, any external claim about these numbers stays regime-conditional (matched, complete telemetry; contended vs uncontended reported separately).

## What changes for our design (bead dr-fol, v2 solver)

**Nothing found supersedes the v2 design.** The work-item-level scheduling problem (feasibility from eligibility/exclusive-resources/capacity, lexicographic objective, full-graph awareness, rolling-horizon CP-SAT) remains unoccupied territory. Specific adjustments, in priority order:

1. **Adopt priority-as-time-offset for the age term (steal from Trigger.dev).** Expressing priority as an arrival-time offset gives starvation-freedom by construction and collapses "priority" and "age" into one dimension; cheaper than a lexicographic age tail and matches our replay evidence that fine-grained ordering beyond bands buys ~nothing. ([Trigger.dev queue docs](https://trigger.dev/docs/queue-concurrency))
2. **Add fairness keys (weighted round-robin across rigs/epics) to contended pools before adding solver sophistication (steal from Temporal).** Our +10% gain lives exactly where Temporal's Fairness feature targets: contended queues monopolized by one source. A fairness-key layer is O(days) to implement on the pull-claim path and should be measured in the same replay harness before the CP-SAT layer lands. ([Temporal docs](https://docs.temporal.io/develop/task-queue-priority-fairness))
3. **Keep the solver off the critical path (steal from Dyserve + Kueue).** Pre-solve dispatch plans at admission/event time at 2-3 load levels, serve claims from the cached plan, residual re-solve on failure preserving committed assignments (Dyserve pattern); structure feasibility as Kueue-style two-phase admission (quota/exclusive-resource reservation, then admission checks). This resolves the CAS-pull vs central-solve tension without making workers wait on CP-SAT.
4. **Human-review-bandwidth resource: pair the capacity model with a creation-time effort predictor.** MSR'26 (2601.00753) shows cheap static features predict review effort at AUC 0.96. Model reviewer capacity as a renewable per-day resource consumed by predicted effort, not PR count. This is also our strongest external-novelty claim, and the predictor is the missing input we did not have a citation for.
5. **Rename/reframe the stochastic-success term.** Per-request success prediction is occupied (SmartLLMs, router literature). The defensible claim is "success-probability-weighted unlock value with rework-cost propagation in a work-item queue." LLMSched's entropy framing (prefer stages that *reduce uncertainty*) suggests an addition: value exploratory/spike beads partly by expected information gain on downstream estimates.
6. **Cohort borrowing for pool capacity (steal from Kueue) is worth a design note**, as the answer to "hot pool starves while sibling pool idles" that neither `gc-capacity --rebalance` nor per-pool CAS addresses structurally.
7. **Watch list**: CQB-MNL queueing bandits (2602.02061) for coupling model-tier routing with queue stability; Temporal Fairness pricing/mechanics as the reference implementation; kubernetes-sigs/agent-sandbox + Volcano AgentCube if we ever move worker execution onto K8s; AgentServeSim's replay-validation methodology (real-vs-sim on identical inter-turn schedules) as a template for hardening our own replay harness.
8. **Do not add**: gang scheduling (single-worker beads), weighted multi-feature scoring (rejected by our own replay and shipped by no one), LLM-as-scheduler (every product doing this treats it as a planner UX, with no evidence of ordering gains; and it violates our ZFC boundary in the wrong direction — dispatch ordering is deterministic-math territory).
